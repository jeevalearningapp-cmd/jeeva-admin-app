# 1. NMC CODE

## **Introduction to the NMC CBT Exam**

Starting a nursing career in the UK requires one essential step: passing the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) Computer-Based Test (CBT).

The CBT is an online exam that checks your core nursing knowledge and clinical reasoning to ensure you can practice safely in UK healthcare. Passing the CBT is mandatory to register with the NMC; without registration you cannot work as a nurse or midwife in the UK.

A graduate nurse watches student Susan Petty prepare a hypodermic syringe.

The NMC CBT is the gateway that confirms aspiring nurses have the core skills and knowledge to deliver safe, effective care.

It protects patients and public trust by ensuring every registrant meets a consistent baseline of competence. The exam tests your ability to apply nursing principles within the UK system, emphasising patient - centred care and professional standards.

## **Understanding the NMC Code**

Logo for the 9th World Congress on Nursing and Healthcare, held in 2020.

The NMC Code is the core standard for nursing and midwifery practice in the UK.

It defines the professional and ethical expectations every nurse must meet. The Code rests on four pillars: Prioritise People, Practice Effectively, Preserve Safety, and Promote Professionalism. Together these pillars shape safe, competent, and compassionate care.

## **The Pillar: Prioritise People**

A healthcare worker in full PPE tends to an elderly patient in a hospital bed.

Prioritise People means nurses put patients’ needs and well‑being first.

It requires treating each person as an individual, respecting their values, beliefs and preferences. A key task is obtaining valid informed consent — giving clear, relevant information about proposed care or treatment and confirming the patient understands before they agree.

Doctor and patient in a consultation booth at a COVID-19 vaccination centre.

Valid consent must be given voluntarily, without coercion or undue influence, and reflect the patient’s genuine choice.

Nurses must assess capacity: can the patient understand the information, retain it long enough to decide, weigh the options, and communicate their decision? When capacity is impaired, nurses advocate for the patient’s rights and preferences, ensuring care remains patient - centred and respects autonomy.

## **Prioritise People: Practical Application**

A doctor examines a young boy with a stethoscope.

Applying the 'Prioritise People' pillar often means accepting tough choices, like a competent patient refusing recommended treatment.

If a patient with full mental capacity declines medication, you must respect that choice while explaining risks and alternatives. Document the refusal, what information you gave, and your capacity assessment in detail.

These records show how your actions followed the NMC Code while protecting the patient's autonomy and dignity.

## **The Pillar: Practice Effectively**

Medical clinicians discuss documents with a patient on a brick porch.

The 'Practice Effectively' pillar requires nurses to base care on current, high-quality evidence.

Nurses must integrate new research into practice and critically appraise findings to keep care safe and effective. Nurses should follow clinical guidelines and protocols derived from robust evidence to standardize care and reduce variation.

Effective practice includes delegating tasks to other qualified team members.

The nurse remains accountable for patient outcomes, so delegation requires assessing the delegate’s competence and the task’s complexity. Continuous professional development is essential; nurses must actively pursue learning to maintain and improve clinical skills.

Ongoing learning keeps practice safe, current, and aligned with professional standards.

## **Practice Effectively: Practical Application**

A physician and nurse attend to a patient at the Public Health Service Tumor Clinic, Marine Hospital, Baltimore.

Delegating a wound dressing to a trained healthcare assistant is appropriate when the nurse has assessed the patient, the wound’s complexity, and the assistant’s demonstrated competence and scope of practice.

The nurse must give clear instructions and ensure the assistant understands the expected outcomes and when to escalate concerns.

The nurse remains accountable for the delegated task and must monitor the patient’s progress and the wound’s response, checking regularly and documenting observations. Consistent oversight and timely escalation protect patient safety and maintain care quality.

## **The Pillar: Preserve Safety**

A healthcare worker in PPE attends to patients in a hospital ward.

The 'Preserve Safety' pillar focuses on protecting people from harm, starting with spotting risks in any clinical setting.

When hazards or incidents are recognised, staff must report them promptly so immediate action can be taken and lessons learned. Core to this pillar are infection-control practices: consistent hand hygiene and correct use of personal protective equipment to prevent healthcare-associated infections and protect patients from pathogens.

Safety extends beyond infection control to include safeguarding vulnerable people.

Safeguarding means spotting signs of abuse or neglect, knowing when someone is at risk, and following the correct reporting steps. Nurses have a legal duty to act: escalate concerns promptly, document clearly, and involve the right services so vulnerable patients are protected and supported.

## **Preserve Safety: Practical Application**

A nurse spotting unexplained bruises or sudden behavioral change in an elderly patient may be seeing signs of abuse.

The immediate duty is to follow the setting’s reporting chain: tell the safeguarding lead or the manager without delay. This response reflects the Care Act 2014, which requires professionals to act when adults are at risk.

Prompt reporting helps protect the person and starts formal enquiries or safeguarding measures.

## **The Pillar: Promote Professionalism**

A healthcare professional writing on a notepad.

The 'Promote Professionalism' pillar requires nurses to be accountable for their actions and to practice only within their scope.

It requires clear, honest communication with patients, colleagues, and other healthcare professionals. Nurses must use social media cautiously: never share patient information and avoid posts that undermine professional integrity.

Every interaction, online or offline, affects public trust in nursing.

Revalidation is the mandatory process nurses use to show the NMC they remain fit to practise.

It hinges on reflective practice: nurses analyse clinical experiences to learn, correct mistakes, and sharpen skills. Completing revalidation keeps your registration active and assures patients and regulators that your knowledge and care standards are current.

## **Promote Professionalism: Practical Application**

A cautionary tale about social media misuse shows why professionalism and ethics matter.

Posting patient details online, even without names, breaches confidentiality and professional standards. Sharing such information destroys patient trust and damages the nurse–patient relationship. The NMC can take disciplinary action for these breaches, including suspension or removal from the register.

Maintain strict boundaries online to protect patient privacy and your professional future.
